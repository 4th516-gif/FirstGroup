Visual direction

This document is static: it describes the look and feel we align with — the kinds of images that typically appear in the first screen of a Google image search for “Sisyphus” (classical painting, mythological art, marble sculpture, and modern interpretations). We do not need to refresh it when search results shift.

What “Sisyphus” search results converge on

  • Figure and boulder — A muscular or strained body against a large stone or sphere; the action is push, carry, or heave uphill.
  • Hill or cliff — Diagonal ground plane, steep slope, or rocky steps; sense of height and effort.
  • Palette — Deep umbers, soot, bronze, and stone grey; firelight or hell-mouth reds in painterly versions; occasional cold sky or marble white in sculpture.
  • Light — Dramatic chiaroscuro (strong light on skin and stone, dark void behind) in oil paintings; even, gallery-like light on white marble.
  • Canon in art history — Titian’s Sisyphus (Prado) and later symbolist works (e.g. Franz Stuck) recur; they set expectations for “serious” treatment of the myth.
  • Sculpture grammar — Twisted torso, weight on one leg, veins and strain — the same visual language as Hellenistic athletic statues.

Reference painting (public domain context): Titian — Sisyphus (Wikipedia) · Museum: Museo del Prado — Sisyphus

How Stonebound applies this

  • Performance hub (dark) — Near-black ground, bronze/amber accent, stone-textured cards; evokes oil painting and underworld fire without copying any single artwork.
  • Nutrition hub (light) — Warm off-white and marble-like surfaces, deep teal accent; same myth, “human figure” side: clarity, flesh, recovery.
  • Type — Condensed uppercase for titles (monumental, inscribed); readable sans or serif for body; mono for citations (inscription / catalog).
  • Motion — Slow, repetitive micro-motion on the landing SVG (boulder and figure) — echo of the eternal task, not entertainment UI noise.
  • No stock-photo pastiche — We borrow structure and temperature from the image canon, not unlicensed crops of famous paintings.
